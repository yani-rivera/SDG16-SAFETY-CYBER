What “cyber-dependent crime” means. In the GI-TOC index these are crimes that can only exist because of computers and networks, where the technology is both the tool and the target: hacking and data theft, ransomware, malware and botnets, and attacks that knock websites offline (DDoS). They differ from cyber-enabled crimes, such as online scams or selling drugs on the internet, which GI-TOC scores under other markets. The score (1–10) is an expert assessment of how large and harmful this criminal market is in or from each country. It is not a count of attacks or victims, so a high score can mean a country is a base for cyber criminals as much as a place whose people are targeted.

What “resilience” means. In the GI-TOC index, resilience is a country’s ability to withstand and disrupt organized crime through its institutions and society. It averages 12 expert-scored indicators (1–10): political leadership and governance, government transparency and accountability, international cooperation, national policies and laws, the judicial system and detention, law enforcement, territorial integrity, anti-money laundering, economic regulatory capacity, victim and witness support, prevention, and the role of non-state actors such as civil society and the media. A high score means strong defenses; it does not measure how much crime there is, or how safe people feel.

What “feel safe walking alone at night” means. Gallup asks adults: “Do you feel safe walking alone at night in the city or area where you live?” The value is the share answering yes (Gallup Global Safety Report 2025). It measures perceived safety on the street, which can differ from both crime levels and institutional strength. A similar question is used for SDG indicator 16.1.4.

Classes split each indicator into thirds (terciles) across the countries that have it, as computed by the bivariate 2.0 runs; dashed lines are the class breaks. Ranks: 1 is the highest value; tied values share a rank. In the scatter plot, cyber-crime scores come in half steps, so many countries share the same value; each dot is nudged up or down by a small random amount (up to about ±0.2) so none hides another. Exact values are in the tooltips and the country card. Grey countries lack one or both values. Boundaries: Natural Earth, Equal Earth projection.
